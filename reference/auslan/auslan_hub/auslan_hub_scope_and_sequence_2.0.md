# **Foundation to Year 6 Auslan Scope and Sequence**

# Teacher Explanation - ACARA v9 Alignment

**Purpose of this Document**

This document explains how the Foundation-Year 6 (F-6) Auslan Scope and Sequence demonstrates a clear developmental continuum aligned with the Australian Curriculum v9 (ACARA).

It is designed to support teachers, curriculum leaders, and school leadership by:

* Making progression across year levels explicit
* Clarifying how skills, grammar, text types, and cultural understanding develop over time
* Providing audit-ready language aligned with ACARA v9 expectations

**A note to those teaching Auslan in later years for the first time. E.g Grade 3 students learning Auslan for the first time.**When Auslan is introduced to students for the first time (e.g in Grade 3), teachers are not expected to begin with the Foundation (F) curriculum.

The Australian Curriculum is designed as a learning continuum, with year-level expectations that recognise students' developmental readiness. By Grade 3, students have the cognitive, social, and learning skills to begin learning Auslan at the Grade 3 level, even when the language is new to them.

In practice, this means teachers can:

* Introduce foundational Auslan concepts such as:
  + the fingerspelling alphabet
  + numbers
  + basic greetings and introductions
  + question forms
  + understanding that Auslan is a complete language with its own grammar and rules
* Teach these basics within age-appropriate contexts that suit Grade 3 learners
* Then move directly into the skills and knowledge outlined for the relevant year level in the curriculum

Grade 3 students are capable of:

* learning language patterns quickly
* noticing visual grammar and facial expressions
* understanding simple cultural concepts
* participating in structured signed interactions

Because of this, teachers do not need to "work up" through Foundation, Year 1 and Year 2 content before accessing Year 3-level learning. Instead, the basics are embedded and taught explicitly as part of Grade 3 learning.

This approach:

* respects students' developmental readiness
* avoids unnecessary repetition or oversimplification
* aligns with how languages are introduced in schools at later year levels
* supports meaningful engagement with the Auslan curriculum from the outset

In short, students can be new to Auslan and still begin learning at Grade 3 level, as long as foundational language elements are taught intentionally and alongside the curriculum skills.

**1. Auslan Learning in ACARA v9 (F-6 Context)**

In ACARA v9, Auslan learning from Foundation to Year 6 is designed to:

* Build foundational communication skills in early years
* Gradually increase linguistic complexity and communicative purpose
* Support students to move from personal expression to more structured texts

**Progression is not based on topic repetition alone, but on how students use language with increasing control, accuracy, and purpose.**

**This scope and sequence has been intentionally designed to reflect that progression.**

**2. What a "Continuum" Looks Like in the F-6 Sequence**

Across Foundation to Year 6, students develop along four interconnected dimensions:

1. Communicative purpose (why students are signing)
2. Linguistic complexity (how language structures are used)
3. Auslan text types (what kinds of texts are created and interpreted)
4. Cultural understanding and identity (how language reflects Deaf experience)

**3. F-6 Continuum Overview by Phase**

**Foundation - Year 1: Establishing Communication**

**Communicative focus**

* Sharing personal information (name, feelings, likes)
* Participating in routines and classroom interactions

**Language development**

* High-frequency vocabulary
* Basic non-manual features (facial expression for emotion and questions)
* Early use of iconic signs and simple depicting signs

**Text types**

* Short personal descriptions
* Simple signed responses

**Cultural understanding**

* Awareness that Auslan is a language
* Recognition that Deaf people use Auslan to communicate

**Years 2-3: Expanding Description and Recount**

**Communicative focus**

* Describing people, places, and routines
* Recounting personal experiences (e.g. weekends, daily routines)

**Language development**

* Increased use of depicting signs and constructed action
* Sequencing and time signs (yesterday, today, last week)
* Developing control of signing space

**Text types**

* Recounts
* Simple narratives
* Descriptions of places and events

**Cultural understanding**

* Introduction to Deaf community members and experiences
* Awareness of differences in signed languages around the world

**Years 4-5: Explaining, Reporting, and Role-Play**

**Communicative focus**

* Explaining ideas and experiences
* Reporting information (e.g. weather, directions)
* Participating in real-world role-play scenarios

**Language development**

* More complex depicting signs and spatial mapping
* Conditional structures (if/then)
* Expanded use of constructed action for characters and viewpoints

**Text types**

* Reports
* Explanations
* Role-plays and interviews

**Cultural understanding**

* **Deaf technology and Deaf history**
* **Lived experiences such as Dinner Table Syndrome**

**Year 6: Consolidation and Preparation for Secondary**

**Communicative focus**

* Sustained presentations and explanations
* Planning, justifying, and persuading
* Engaging with community and education-related topics

**Language development**

* Extended clauses and cohesive sequencing
* Audience awareness
* Purposeful use of NMFs for emphasis and persuasion

**Text types**

* Informative presentations
* Translations between Auslan and English
* Persuasive signed texts

**Cultural understanding**

* Deaf education and accessibility
* Reflection on Deaf and hearing perspectives

**Year 6 functions as a bridge year, preparing students for the increased linguistic and cognitive demands of secondary Auslan learning.**

**4. Alignment with ACARA v9 Requirements**

**This F-6 Scope and Sequence:**

✔ Demonstrates a clear learning continuum across year levels

✔ Aligns with ACARA v9 content descriptions and elaborations

✔ Builds toward the Year 6 achievement standards

✔ Integrates receptive, expressive, and interactive language use

✔ Explicitly develops Auslan grammar (DS, CA, NMFs, spatial use)

✔ Embeds Deaf culture and identity learning progressively

**Concepts such as weather, animals, routines, and travel are intentionally revisited with increasing linguistic and cognitive complexity, rather than repeated at the same level.**

**5. Audit-Ready Clarification (For Schools)**

**This scope and sequence is not topic-based repetition. It is a structured, developmental program that:**

* Revisits familiar contexts to deepen language control
* Introduces new grammatical features at appropriate stages
* Expands text types and communicative purposes over time

This approach reflects best practice in second language learning and is consistent with ACARA v9 design principles.

**6. How Teachers Can Use This Document**

Teachers can use this explanation to:

* Support curriculum planning and reporting
* Justify Auslan programs during audits or reviews
* Explain progression to leadership and families
* Ensure teaching focuses on skill development, not just vocabulary coverage

**Final Statement**

The Foundation-Year 6 Auslan Scope and Sequence demonstrates a clear, coherent continuum aligned with ACARA v9. It supports students to develop meaningful communication skills, grammatical control, cultural understanding, and readiness for secondary Auslan learning

**Year 7-10 Auslan Scope and Sequence**

**Teacher Explanation - ACARA v9 Alignment**

**Purpose of this Document**

This document explains how the Year 7-10 Auslan Scope and Sequence aligns with the Australian Curriculum v9 (ACARA) and provides teachers and school leaders with confidence that the program demonstrates a clear, defensible learning continuum.

This scope and sequence is written for the 7-10 Sequence pathway, designed specifically for students who begin learning Auslan in Year 7 with little or no prior experience.

**1. Auslan Pathways in ACARA v9**

ACARA v9 recognises that Auslan learners enter the language at different stages. As a result, Auslan is organised into distinct learning pathways, including:

* **F-10 Sequence** (continuous learning from Foundation)
* **7-10 Sequence** (students commencing Auslan in Year 7)

The 7-10 Sequence is not expected to replicate or compress F-10 learning. Instead, it has its own progression and achievement standards that describe expected learning by the end of Year 10.

This scope and sequence has been intentionally designed to align with that expectation.

**2. What "Continuum" Means in the 7-10 Sequence**

In the ACARA v9 Auslan curriculum, progression is demonstrated through increasing communicative demand, linguistic complexity, and text sophistication, rather than through repetition of vocabulary themes.

The Year 7-10 continuum develops across four key areas:

**A. Communicative Demand**

Students progressively move from simple exchanges to sustained, purposeful communication.

* **Year 7** - Exchange personal information using modelled and formulaic language
* **Year 8** - Describe, compare, and explain familiar experiences
* **Year 9** - Engage in sustained conversations; recount and plan
* **Year 10** - Present, translate, and reflect on issues, identity, and community

This progression directly reflects the structure of the Year 10 achievement standards in ACARA v9.

**B. Linguistic Complexity**

Across Years 7-10, students are explicitly scaffolded to move from:

* Individual signs → clauses and linked ideas
* Literal signing → depicting signs (DS) and constructed action (CA)
* Simple statements → questions, interviews, explanations, and persuasion
* Concrete topics → abstract concepts such as identity, culture, and advocacy

This aligns with ACARA v9 expectations around the development of grammatical knowledge, signing space, non-manual features, and text cohesion.

**C. Text Types and Purpose**

Students engage with an increasing range of Auslan text types, each with a clear communicative purpose:

|  |  |
| --- | --- |
| **Year Level** | **Typical Auslan Text Types** |
| Year 7 | Introductions, profiles, short dialogues |
| Year 8 | Descriptions, recounts, narratives |
| Year 9 | Plans, explanations, role-plays |
| Year 10 | Interviews, translations, persuasive texts |

This reflects ACARA v9's emphasis on **audience, purpose, and context**.

**D. Deaf Culture, Identity, and Reflection**

ACARA v9 requires that Auslan learning includes an understanding of:

* Deaf community practices
* Deaf history and lived experiences
* Identity and perspectives
* The role of Auslan in society

Across the 7-10 Sequence, cultural learning develops from:

* Awareness and participation (Year 7)
* Comparison and explanation (Year 8-9)
* Reflection, advocacy, and perspective-taking (Year 10)

**3. How This Scope and Sequence Meets ACARA v9 Requirements**

This Year 7-10 Auslan Scope and Sequence:

✔ Is explicitly designed for the 7-10 Sequence learner pathway

✔ Demonstrates a clear developmental continuum across year levels

✔ Aligns with content descriptions and elaborations in ACARA v9

✔ Builds toward the Year 10 achievement standards

✔ Integrates language use, grammar, text creation, translation, and reflection

✔ Embeds Deaf culture, history, and identity throughout

Importantly, learning is scaffolded across years, not repeated. Topics such as food, travel, or identity are revisited with increasing linguistic and cognitive complexity, as required by ACARA.

**4. Common Misconceptions (Clarified)**

This scope and sequence does **not**:

* Treat Year 7-10 as a compressed F-10 pathway
* Rely on vocabulary lists without grammatical progression
* Assume prior Auslan knowledge
* Repeat the same learning expectations each year

Instead, it reflects best practice for second language learners beginning Auslan in secondary school.

**5. How Teachers Can Use This Document**

Teachers can use this explanation to:

* Support curriculum planning and documentation
* Justify Auslan programs to school leadership
* Respond confidently to curriculum audits or reviews
* Communicate the value and rigour of Auslan learning to families
