Suggested non-fiction text (lesson 7)
The Southern Cross: A Bright Star Story
Look up at the night sky and you will see many twinkling stars. One special group is the Southern Cross. It looks like a kite or cross and is very famous in Australia because we can see it so clearly.
For thousands of years, Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander peoples have looked at the Southern Cross and found stories and signs. They saw animals and patterns in the stars that helped them understand the world. The Southern Cross, and the dark patches around it, appear in many Dreaming stories. These stories teach lessons about life, the land, and family. For some groups, the dark patches form the body and legs of a giant emu. The stars also acted like a map, guiding people as they travelled.
The Southern Cross is part of the Milky Way – our galaxy. Its stars are very far from us, and even further from each other. Learning about the Southern Cross shows us how vast and wonderful space is.
Where to See the Southern Cross Best
To see the Southern Cross clearly, you need a dark sky far away from city lights. Australia has special areas called “Dark Sky Parks” or “Dark Sky Sanctuaries.” These places are protected so that there is no light pollution, making the skies very clear and dark. For example, Warrumbungle National Park in New South Wales, Arkaroola Dark Sky Sanctuary in South Australia and Uluru in the Northern Territory are all amazing places for stargazing.
