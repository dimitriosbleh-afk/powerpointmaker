===== SLIDE 2 =====
  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.

===== SLIDE 3 =====
  Use of this resource
  Please download the accompanying Literature Study Guide that matches this unit.
  Read the novel and familiarise yourself with the characters, themes and literary features.
  This resource is designed to be modified and adapted to suit the individual context and needs of each school, teacher and cohort of students.
  It is recommended that teachers read the lesson detail provided in the Literature Study Guide in preparation for delivering this lesson. This may include:
  identifying which components of the lesson may need adapting, depending on the lesson time available
  pre-reading the specified novel pages to understand key themes, events and big ideas
  deciding on suitable pause points relevant to students comprehension abilities/needs and which queries will be asked to facilitate class discussion and overall comprehension of the text
  the incidental vocabulary that may need to be taught during reading
  any chosen literary devices that want to be explored
  identifying which sentence-level and text-level writing booklet tasks will be completed
  Learning outcomes have been designed to be repeated across a unit of work. Students may initially take longer when first introduced to new concepts and related booklet tasks. As these are repeated across the allocated period, it is anticipated that the pace of each lesson should increase as students become more confident with newly acquired skills and knowledge.

===== SLIDE 14 =====
  Which sentence is correct?
  Sara shuddered the door when she heard the thunder rumbling.
  Sara felt a shudder when she heard the thunder rumbling in the distance.
  W
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  Option 1 is incorrect because "shudder" is a verb that means to tremble or shake, typically as a result of fear, cold, or revulsion. It describes a physical reaction or movement, not an action that can be performed on an object like a door. Option 2 shows the correct use of shudder to mean shake suddenly from fear or cold

===== SLIDE 26 =====
  Sentence-level writing
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  Full instructional cycles (I do, We do, Check for Understanding, You do) are provided in each lesson, despite the sentence level writing strategies being repeated. This ensures
  teachers have all of the necessary resources specific to the content in those pages of the novel. Sentence level writing tasks are designed to serve two purposes:
  Explicitly teach students to craft a variety of sentence types and structures in order to develop their written expression
  Provide students with opportunities to write about what they read in order to demonstrate comprehension of the text.
