===== SLIDE 2 =====
  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Read aloud or summarise before opening the novel for the first time.
  
  SENSITIVITY ADVISORY:
  What it is: Storm Boy contains Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander content. Older language may not match how we speak today.
  Framing language: The story shows respect for the Coorong and the Ngarrindjeri people.
  Watch for: students who may be affected by names or images of deceased persons.
  Protocol: pause if a student is upset, offer a quiet break with a peer or aide, follow up at recess.

  pre-reading the specified novel pages to understand key themes, events and big ideas

===== SLIDE 11 =====
  Read Aloud
  Storm Boy pp.5-9
  Reading Mode: Teacher Read Aloud
  Listen carefully and picture the setting in your mind
  Think about: Where does Storm Boy live? What is it like? Who does he live with?
  I will pause at key moments to check your understanding
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  I am going to read pp.5-9 aloud.
  Listen for: where does Storm Boy live? Who does he live with?
  I will pause to check.
  
  DO:
  Read aloud, slow and clear.
  Pause at "wet underbelly" (p.5), "like snakes" (p.6) and "but Storm Boy" (p.6).
  After each pause: 10 seconds think, 20 seconds partner talk, cold call 1-2 pairs.
  
  CFU CHECKPOINT:
  Technique: Think-Pair-Share
  Script:
  Ask: What is the author describing when they say wet underbelly? Expected: the underside of the land where it meets the sea.
  Scan for: students linking it to land near the water.
  PROCEED:
  >=80% can describe the setting in their own words. Continue reading.
  PIVOT:
  Most likely: students take "underbelly" literally.
  Reteach: hold a hand up, palm down. "Underbelly is the soft side underneath. The land near the sea is like the underbelly of Australia."
  Re-check: "Is the wet underbelly the sky or the land near the water?"
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Name "like snakes" as a simile briefly. Deeper craft work comes later.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students worried about real snakes - clarify: comparison only, no real snakes.
  Students who cannot recall any details - check in during partner talk.

  Write an expanded sentence
  Simple sentence: “They lived here.”
  (Image)
  Who
  What (doing)
  When
  Where
  Storm Boy and Hide-Away Tom
  lived
  after they left Adelaide
  in a little humpy between Coorong and the sea
  After they left Adelaide, Storm Boy and Hide-Away Tom lived in a little humpy between
  Image credit: Pixabay
  Coorong and the sea.
  I
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  Watch this. My kernel sentence: "They lived here." That is correct, but it tells me nothing.
  I need to ask: WHO, WHAT DOING, WHEN, WHERE.
  WHO - Storm Boy and Hide-Away Tom. WHAT DOING - lived. WHEN - after they left Adelaide. WHERE - in a humpy between the Coorong and the sea.
  I am going to put the WHEN at the front: "After they left Adelaide, Storm Boy and Hide-Away Tom lived in a little humpy between the Coorong and the sea."
  Notice the comma after the when detail at the front.
  
  DO:
  Display the kernel and the table.
  Fill in each column as you think aloud.
  Build the expanded sentence under the table.
  Point clearly to the comma.
  
  MISCONCEPTIONS:
  Misconception: students think expanding means adding more adjectives.
    Why: prior "juicy words" approaches.
    Impact: padded sentences with no new information.
    Quick correction: "Expanding answers WHO, WHEN, WHERE, WHY. Not fancy words."
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Core I Do. Fronted adverbial taught explicitly with the comma rule.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students unsure about "adverbial" - reframe: "a group of words that tells when, where or how."
