  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Flag before reading. The novel and surrounding resources reference First Nations characters.
  
  SENSITIVITY ADVISORY:
  What it is: First Nations content in the novel.
  Framing language: 'We talk about First Nations characters with respect.'
  Watch for: any student unsettled by period language.
  Protocol: pause, name the language as historical, allow students to step out.

  Use of this resource
  Please download the accompanying Literature Study Guide that matches this unit.
  Read the novel and familiarise yourself with the characters, themes and literary features.
  This resource is designed to be modified and adapted to suit the individual context and needs of each school, teacher and cohort of students.
  It is recommended that teachers read the lesson detail provided in the Literature Study Guide in preparation for delivering this lesson. This may include:
  identifying which components of the lesson may need adapting, depending on the lesson time available
  pre-reading the specified novel pages to understand key themes, events and big ideas
  deciding on suitable pause points relevant to students comprehension abilities/needs and which queries will be asked to facilitate class discussion and overall comprehension of the text
  the incidental vocabulary that may need to be taught during reading
  any chosen literary devices that want to be explored
  identifying which sentence-level and text-level writing booklet tasks will be completed
  Learning outcomes have been designed to be repeated across a unit of work. Students may initially take longer when first introduced to new concepts and related booklet tasks. As these are repeated across the allocated period, it is anticipated that the pace of each lesson should increase as students become more confident with newly acquired skills and knowledge.

===== SLIDE 29 =====
  Literary Device
  Personification
  Personification gives human actions or feelings to animals or objects
  "the tall birds stood up and clapped and cheered the rising sun" (p. 16)
  The birds cannot really clap or cheer -- the author makes them sound like people celebrating
  This makes us feel that the birds are alive and joyful, and the Coorong is a magical place
  Storm Boy | Lesson 6 of 30 | Week 2 | Year 3/4 Literacy
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  This part of the book uses a writing trick called personification.
  Personification gives human actions or feelings to animals or objects.
  Read with me: 'the tall birds stood up and clapped and cheered the rising sun.'
  Ask: Can birds really clap and cheer? Expected: no - the author makes them sound like people celebrating.
  
  DO:
  Point to the example sentence as students read.
  Mime the clapping and cheering.
  Cold call: 'What human action did the author give the birds?'
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Light literary device touch under three minutes. Anchors why writers expand sentences with vivid detail.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who think personification is only cartoon talking animals. Clarify: small actions count too.
  
  Stage: Literary Device I Do | VTLM 2.0: Explicit Teaching

===== SLIDE 30 =====
  Sentence expansion
  We expand sentences because:
  It gives more information to the reader.
  It makes a sentence more interesting and easier to read.
  It helps us write like writers.
  I
  Image credit: Pixabay
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  Watch this first.
  We expand sentences for three reasons: more information, more interesting, more like real writers.
  'Storm Boy walked' is a kernel - true but empty.
  'One morning, Storm Boy walked along the beach because he wanted to see the pelicans' answers when, where and why.
  This is the trap: just adding adjectives is not enough.
  
  DO:
  Point to each reason as you read it.
  Write kernel and expanded version side by side on the board.
  Cold call: 'Which one tells you more?'
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  This is the I Do anchor. Students must see the contrast before they try.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who think expanding means more adjectives. Emphasise: we answer when, where, why, how.

===== SLIDE 34 =====
  Adverbials
  An adverbial is a group of words that describes a verb.
  Examples
  The visitors hurt the birds by sneaking into the sanctuary.
  The birds splashed in the early morning.
  Storm Boy went walking along the beach.
  If you use a group of words to describe how, when or where in an expanded sentence, you are using an adverbial.
  I
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  An adverbial is a group of words that describes a verb.
  Example one: 'The visitors hurt the birds by sneaking into the sanctuary' - this tells us how.
  Example two: 'The birds splashed in the early morning' - tells us when.
  Example three: 'Storm Boy went walking along the beach' - tells us where.
  If you describe how, when or where with a group of words, you are using an adverbial.
  
  DO:
  Highlight the adverbial in each example.
  Cold call one student per example: 'How, when or where?'
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Only run if students need adverbial revision. Adverbial of manner = how, time = when, place = where.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who confuse adverbial with adjective. Adverbial describes the action.
  
  Stage: I Do (optional) | VTLM 2.0: Explicit Teaching

  Expand Your Sentences
  First: Read the question carefully
  Next: Identify which details (who, when, where, how, why) will help you answer it
  Then: Expand the kernel sentence by adding those details
  Challenge: Try starting at least one sentence with a WHEN or WHERE detail at the front -- remember the comma!
  Storm Boy | Lesson 6 of 30 | Week 2 | Year 3/4 Literacy
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  Your turn to expand sentences on your own.
  First: read the question carefully.
  Next: pick which details (who, when, where, how, why) help answer it.
  Then: write your expanded sentence.
  Challenge: try one sentence with a WHEN or WHERE at the front - and the comma.
  
  DO:
  Distribute or open booklets.
  Set fifteen minutes.
  Circulate. Check the first sentence each student writes.
  
  ENABLING & EXTENDING:
  ENABLING PROMPT:
  Task: five-column table with question word labels (who, when, where, how, why) to fill in before writing.
  Extra Notes: stay with three details rather than four.
  EXTENDING PROMPT:
  Task: write the expanded sentence and a second version starting with a different question word.
  Extra Notes: praise variety in starters.
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Main check on SC3. Mark which students get this on the first sentence.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who write the kernel only. They've lost the task.
  Students adding details that don't relate to the question. Reread the question with them.
