===== SLIDE 2 =====
  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.

===== SLIDE 3 =====
  Use of this resource
  Please download the accompanying Literature Study Guide that matches this unit.
  Read the novel and familiarise yourself with the characters, themes and literary features.
  This resource is designed to be modified and adapted to suit the individual context and needs of each school, teacher and cohort of students.
  It is recommended that teachers read the lesson detail provided in the Literature Study Guide in preparation for delivering this lesson. This may include:
  identifying which components of the lesson may need adapting, depending on the lesson time available
  pre-reading the specified novel pages to understand key themes, events and big ideas
  deciding on suitable pause points relevant to students comprehension abilities/needs and which queries will be asked to facilitate class discussion and overall comprehension of the text
  the incidental vocabulary that may need to be taught during reading
  any chosen literary devices that want to be explored
  identifying which sentence-level and text-level writing booklet tasks will be completed
  Learning outcomes have been designed to be repeated across a unit of work. Students may initially take longer when first introduced to new concepts and related booklet tasks. As these are repeated across the allocated period, it is anticipated that the pace of each lesson should increase as students become more confident with newly acquired skills and knowledge.

===== SLIDE 7 =====
  Learning objectives
  We are learning to
  read and comprehend the text using knowledge of text structures and language, and by monitoring comprehension.
  ask and answer questions to clarify meaning and promote deeper understanding of the text.
  identify and describe how literary devices shape meaning in the text.
  learn and apply newly taught vocabulary.
  summarise the main ideas in the text by creating a single paragraph outline (SPO).

  flung
  Familiar context
  Definition
  To fling something is to throw it, usually forcefully or angrily.
  Word associations
  Image credit: Unsplash
  I
  Part of Speech
  verb
  Other contexts for use
  He flung himself down on his bunk and sobbed softly to himself.
  Sam flung the frisbee across the park, watching it soar through the air towards his friend.
  fling, throw, chuck
  Affixes
  fling, flinging
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SOURCES:
  Macquarie Dictionary, 2024.
  
  SAY:
  Our first word is flung.
  Watch this first - I am going to think aloud.
  To fling something is to throw it, usually forcefully or angrily.
  In the story, Storm Boy flung himself down on his bunk and sobbed softly. So flung is a strong, overwhelmed motion - he was so upset he did not just lie down.
  Say the word with me... flung!
  
  DO:
  Point to the image on the slide.
  Use a quick, sharp throwing gesture as you say flung.
  Have students repeat the word twice.
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Connect the strong-throw motion to the story moment - Storm Boy is overwhelmed when he flings himself down.
  
  ENABLING & EXTENDING:
  ENABLING PROMPT:
  Task: Repeat the word and copy the throwing gesture.
  EXTENDING PROMPT:
  Task: Identify one other moment in Storm Boy where someone could fling something.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who think flung means a gentle throw - confirm it is forceful or careless.
  
  Stage: Vocabulary I Do | VTLM: Explicit Teaching

  Sentence building

  her dad gave her a big hug.
  She was sobbing because
  she accidentally let go of her balloon.
  CfU
  Image credit: Pixabay
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  Sentence building. Read each one with me.
  She was sobbing, but her friend quickly brought her a new balloon.
  She was sobbing, so her dad gave her a big hug.
  She was sobbing because she accidentally let go of her balloon.
  Ask: what does each connector tell us? Expected: but flips the idea, so shows a result, because gives the reason.
  
  DO:
  Read each sentence aloud.
  Point to each connector word.
  
  CFU CHECKPOINT:
  Technique: Class discussion plus targeted question
  Script:
  Which connector shows a reason? Cold call.
  Scan for: students saying because.
  PROCEED:
  If students name the connectors correctly, move to clutching.
  PIVOT:
  Most likely misconception: students think but and so are interchangeable.
  Reteach: but flips the idea, so is the result, because is the reason.
  Fresh re-check: She was sobbing ____ she missed her friend - which connector?
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  End-of-word CFU using the connector grammar - sets up sentence-construction work later.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who only read the sentences without thinking about the connectors - prompt them to focus on the joining word.
