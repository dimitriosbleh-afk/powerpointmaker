===== SLIDE 2 =====
  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.

  identifying which components of the lesson may need adapting, depending on the lesson time available
  pre-reading the specified novel pages to understand key themes, events and big ideas

  All students respond to the teacher’s question, showing their responses at the same time.
  Non-volunteers

===== SLIDE 7 =====
  Learning objectives
  We are learning to
  understand the purpose, structure and features of an information report.
  understand the purpose and structure of conclusion for an information report.
  plan an information report by drawing on texts read, viewed and listened to for ideas.
  research, summarise and list topic-related ideas when planning.
  write a conclusion for an information report.
  use language features of an information report in a conclusion (e.g. present tense, nouns and noun groups, third person pronouns, adjectives to describe, classify, compare or quantify, adverbials – place, time, manner).
  proofread, revise and edit introduction to refine language, correct spelling and ensure cohesion and engagement for the reader.

  This report detailed the location of the Coorong and its cultural and ecological significance. (T) The importance of this expansive wetland area is enormous for both past and present-day people, flora, and fauna. (S) The Coorong, a long shallow lagoon filled with life, is truly an iconic Australian landscape! (G)
  The Coorong is a narrow, shallow lagoon that stretches across the Limestone coast in South Australia. (G) It is an expansive area of wetlands and waterways that is both culturally and ecologically important. (S) This report will discuss exactly what the Coorong is, where it is located, its cultural significance, and the flora and fauna that rely on it for their survival. (T)
  Introduction
  Conclusion
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  The introduction uses GST - General, Specific, Thesis.
  The conclusion uses TSG - Thesis, Specific, General.
  Read both with me - introduction first, then conclusion.
  Notice the mirror - the introduction starts wide and zooms in. The conclusion starts on what we discussed and zooms back out.
  Ask: what is the purpose of the thesis statement? What does the specific statement tell us? What do you notice about the general statement?
