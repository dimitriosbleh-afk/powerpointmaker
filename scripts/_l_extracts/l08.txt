===== SLIDE 3 =====
  Use of this resource
  Please download the accompanying Literature Study Guide that matches this unit.
  Read the novel and familiarise yourself with the characters, themes and literary features.
  This resource is designed to be modified and adapted to suit the individual context and needs of each school, teacher and cohort of students.
  It is recommended that teachers read the lesson detail provided in the Literature Study Guide in preparation for delivering this lesson. This may include:
  identifying which components of the lesson may need adapting, depending on the lesson time available
  pre-reading the specified novel pages to understand key themes, events and big ideas
  deciding on suitable pause points relevant to students comprehension abilities/needs and which queries will be asked to facilitate class discussion and overall comprehension of the text
  the incidental vocabulary that may need to be taught during reading
  any chosen literary devices that want to be explored
  identifying which sentence-level and text-level writing booklet tasks will be completed
  Learning outcomes have been designed to be repeated across a unit of work. Students may initially take longer when first introduced to new concepts and related booklet tasks. As these are repeated across the allocated period, it is anticipated that the pace of each lesson should increase as students become more confident with newly acquired skills and knowledge.

===== SLIDE 5 =====
  Whiteboard response
  All students respond to the teacher’s question, showing their responses at the same time.
  Non-volunteers
  The teacher asks a question, gives wait time, then calls on individual students to respond.
  Independent work
  Students respond to a prompt in their exercise book or booklet.
  Pair share
  Students discuss with the person next to them.
  Choral response
  Students respond orally all together.
  Thumbs up, thumbs down
  Students indicate whether they agree or disagree with a statement, or whether something is an example or non-example.
  Engagement icons - to go in top right corner
  Class discussion
  Students discuss as a class and share ideas
  Show fingers
  Students indicate using their fingers which is the correct answer. Ideal for multiple choice answers

===== SLIDE 31 =====
  Keywords and Phrases
  Keywords and phrases are the parts of the sentence that tell us the main idea.
  Often the keywords tell us the who/what, what it did, when, where, why and how.
  I
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  Keywords and phrases tell us the main idea.
  They usually answer who/what, what it did, when, where, why and how.
  The other words are joiners and extras.
  
  DO:
  Point to each piece of information as you read it.
  Write a quick example on the board: 'The dog barked loudly at the postman.' Underline: dog, barked, postman.
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  I Do for keyword identification. Move slowly so students see what gets LEFT OUT.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who underline every word. They need to see what gets cut.
  
  Stage: I Do | VTLM 2.0: Explicit Teaching
