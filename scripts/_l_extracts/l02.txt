  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.

===== SLIDE 34 =====
  Conjunctions ‘because’, ‘but’, ‘so’
  shows why something has happened
  because
  but
  shows a change in direction
  so
  shows cause and effect
  I
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  Three new conjunctions: because, but, so.
  BECAUSE shows WHY something happened. It gives a reason.
  BUT shows a change in direction. Something unexpected.
  SO shows what happens as a result. Cause and effect.
  
  DO:
  Read each definition.
  Quick everyday model on the board: "I was hungry BECAUSE I did not eat lunch. I was hungry, BUT I had no food. I was hungry, SO I ate an apple."
  Highlight comma rule: comma before BUT and SO. No comma before BECAUSE.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students confusing BECAUSE and SO - say: "Because answers WHY. So answers WHAT happened next."

===== SLIDE 35 =====
  Sentence stem:
  "Fingerbone loved the Coorong"
  Fingerbone loved the Coorong because he felt a strong connection to the land.
  Fingerbone loved the Coorong, but but he did not love the tiger snakes who lived there.
  Fingerbone loved the Coorong, so he taught Storm Boy how to understand and respect it.
  Fingerbone loved the Coorong because
  Fingerbone loved the Coorong, but
  Fingerbone loved the Coorong, so
  I
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  Watch this. My stem: "Fingerbone loved the Coorong."
  BECAUSE version: "Fingerbone loved the Coorong because he felt a strong connection to the land." Because tells me WHY.
  BUT version: "Fingerbone loved the Coorong, but he did not love the tiger snakes." But shows a change.
  SO version: "Fingerbone loved the Coorong, so he taught Storm Boy to understand it." So shows the result.
  Comma before BUT and SO. No comma before BECAUSE.
  
  DO:
  Display the stem.
  Build each version one at a time.
  Point to comma placement.
  Ask: Did Fingerbone love the Coorong in all three? Yes. What changed? The second part.
  
  MISCONCEPTIONS:
  Misconception: students put a comma before because.
    Why: overgeneralisation from but and so.
    Impact: incorrect punctuation in most contexts.
    Quick correction: "No comma before because. Just before but and so."
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Core I Do. The repeated stem makes the meaning shift visible.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students saying all three sentences mean the same - re-read the second clauses with extra emphasis.
