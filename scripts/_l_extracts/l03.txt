===== SLIDE 3 =====
  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Read aloud or summarise before today's reading.
  
  SENSITIVITY ADVISORY:
  What it is: Storm Boy contains Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander content. Older language may not match how we speak today.
  Framing language: "This story shows respect for the Coorong and the Ngarrindjeri people."
  Watch for: students affected by names or images of deceased persons.
  Protocol: pause, offer a quiet break, follow up at recess.

  pre-reading the specified novel pages to understand key themes, events and big ideas

===== SLIDE 6 =====
  Whiteboard response
  All students respond to the teacher’s question, showing their responses at the same time.
  Non-volunteers
  The teacher asks a question, gives wait time, then calls on individual students to respond.
  Independent work
  Students respond to a prompt in their exercise book or booklet.
  Pair share
  Students discuss with the person next to them.
  Choral response
  Students respond orally all together.
  Thumbs up, thumbs down
  Students indicate whether they agree or disagree with a statement, or whether something is an example or non-example.
  Engagement icons - to go in top right corner
  Class discussion
  Students discuss as a class and share ideas
  Show fingers
  Students indicate using their fingers which is the correct answer. Ideal for multiple choice answers

  A summary is a short version of a story or event that includes the most important parts. It doesn't include extra details, just the main ideas, so it's easy to understand.
  What is a summary?
  I
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---
  SAY:
  A summary is a short version. Most important parts only. No extra detail.
  Watch this. I am going to summarise a passage from p.14.
  I ask: WHO - Storm Boy. WHAT DOING - wandered, finding treasures. WHEN - after a big blow. WHERE - the beach near the Coorong.
  I am going to put the WHEN at the front: "After a big blow, Storm Boy wandered along the beach near the Coorong, finding all kinds of treasure."
  Notice: I did NOT include every detail. I captured the main idea.
  
  DO:

  Work through the prompts visibly.

  WATCH FOR:
  Students wanting every detail - redirect: "In ONE sentence. What is the MAIN idea?"
  Students writing too short - push: "Add detail. When? Where? What kinds of things?"
