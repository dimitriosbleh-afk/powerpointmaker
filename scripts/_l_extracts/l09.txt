===== SLIDE 2 =====
  Cultural sensitivity
  The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures.
  Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
  Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
  Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
  Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.
  --- EXISTING NOTES ---

  Cultural significance of the Coorong
  The Coorong holds immense cultural significance for the Ngarrindjeri people. The area is known as ‘Kurangk,’ meaning ‘long narrow neck,’ in the Ngarrindjeri language. The Ngarrindjeri's cultural heritage involvement is integral to the park's current management. The Coorong has culturally significant sites such as middens. A midden is a site where Indigenous communities camped and left the remains of their meals. Oyster and cockle shells, as well as animal bones, are often found in middens. Some middens also contain tools made from stone, bone, or shell. At some sites, very large deposits grew over time. Some middens can be up to a few metres deep.

  What it is and where it is located
  An integral part of the Murray-Darling basin, the Coorong is located at the end of the Murray River. It is more than 100 km long and is separated from the ocean by a length of sandy shore named the Younghusband Peninsula. It is a lagoon that contains a mix of freshwater, brackish water, and saltwater across its various stretches of water. This is because it sits between the salty water of the ocean and the freshwater rivers of the basin. The Coorong is a large expanse of different types of water that spans the South-Eastern coast of South Australia.

  Task: sentence frame - 'Overall, the Coorong is ___ and is located ___.'
  Extra Notes: students use their topic sentence as a guide.
  EXTENDING PROMPT:
  Task: write a CS that uses different wording but says the same big idea.
  Extra Notes: praise paraphrasing.
  
  TEACHER NOTES:
  Final You Do task - completes the SPO. Students will write the full body paragraph in lesson 10.
  
  WATCH FOR:
  Students who copy the topic sentence verbatim. Prompt to paraphrase.
  Students who add new facts. Cue: 'No new ideas in the CS.'
