Cultural sensitivity
The following resources explore Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander histories and cultures. 
Note that the Australian Curriculum and State variants use the terminology of Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples and First Nations Australians. This language has been adopted across these resources, and can also be altered to suit your local context.
Please be aware of the cultural sensitivity of students in your class prior to reading.
Some material from the texts studied may contain words, descriptions and terms which may be culturally sensitive. Students should be made aware that this language may reflect the views of the period in which the content was created or set, but may not be considered appropriate today.
Aboriginal and Torres Strait Islander Peoples should be aware that some reading/viewing material may contain images, voices and names of deceased persons.

SAY:
'Watch this first.'
'The word is wounded. Say it with me. Wounded.'
'Wounded means hurt or injured. It is an adjective, so it describes a person, animal or thing.'

TEACHER NOTES:
Keep the focus on the adjective use. The boat is being described like an injured animal.

MISCONCEPTIONS:
Misconception: wounded and wound mean the same thing.
  Why: shared spelling root.
  Impact: students misuse wounded as a verb of action.
  Quick correction: 'Wounded describes how someone is. A wound is the actual injury.'

WATCH FOR:
Students confusing wounded with wound; address only if it surfaces.

Which word is unlike wounded?
sore
tired
injured
hurting
CfU
[NOTES]
SAY:
'Three of these words are similar to wounded. One is unlike wounded.'
Ask: 'Show me your fingers - one for sore, two for tired, three for injured, four for hurting. Three, two, one, show.' Expected: 2 (tired).

DO:
Scan responses.
Take one student's reasoning before revealing.

CFU CHECKPOINT:
Technique: Show Fingers 1-4
Script:
'Show me which word does not fit with wounded. Three, two, one.'
Scan for: a clear majority on 2 (tired).
PROCEED:
Confirm tired and remind class that sore, injured and hurting all describe being hurt.
PIVOT:
Most likely misconception: students confuse tired (low energy) with hurting (in pain).
Reteach with a quick example: 'After running, I am tired. After falling, I am hurting - my body has been wounded.'
Re-check: 'Show me again - which is unlike wounded?'

TEACHER NOTES:
Word-association is the threshold check before the build-a-sentence task.

WATCH FOR:
Students choosing sore as the odd one out; address that sore still belongs to the hurt family.
